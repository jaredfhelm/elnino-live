What changed since the first note

The response catalogs and price feeds have been extended to the three assets the 2026-07-10 note named as the highest-value expansion of this system. The board now ranks 11 theses instead of 8. Fishmeal enters LONG (confidence 79, medium-horizon expected +83%, target probability 56%), the highest-ranked of the three and consistent with paper 18's verdict that the Peruvian anchoveta chain — upwelling collapse to quota cancellation — is the single most repeatable signal in the corpus, present in five of five events. Palm oil enters LONG (64; +44%, P74%) on paper 10's lag-driven mechanism, and rice enters LONG (53) on paper 11's monsoon-and-policy chain.

Each new thesis now carries a real primary-source price feed rather than an honest gap: fishmeal the IMF benchmark (FRED PFISHUSDM, monthly 1992 to 2026), palm oil the IMF USD benchmark (PPOILUSDM), and rice the World Bank 'Pink Sheet' Thai 5% series (1960 to 2026). Fishmeal and palm oil do not trade as free exchange futures, so these benchmark series — the ones the papers themselves cite — are the honest substitute; rice uses the World Bank benchmark whose deep history covers every documented event.

What the primary sources corrected

Extending these assets surfaced three magnitude corrections the seeds now carry, each flagged in the response notes. Fishmeal's headline moves are benchmark-dependent: the IMF and World Bank series show smaller USD moves — roughly +25% to +60% for the modern events — than the FOB Peru spot quotes that made headlines, and the 1997-98 rise on the IMF series was about +25% to +40%, not a doubling. Palm oil's famous 1997-98 spike was largely ringgit devaluation; in USD the move was about +30% to +50% before a collapse to $280 by mid-1999. Rice is policy-amplified: paper 11 and the 2023 episode show the El Niño signal alone is about +5% to +10%, and an Indian export ban pushes it to +18% to +25%.

One live note for the reader: fishmeal is not a historical curiosity right now. The IMF series sits at an all-time high in mid-2026 (above 2,400, past the 2014 record) with Peru's season suspended — the corpus's most deterministic chain is firing in the present, which is precisely why the first note argued it belonged on the board.

What did not change — the caveats still stand

Adding the Tier-1 assets deepened the response catalog exactly as the first note recommended; it did not change the honest verdict. Everything on the board remains unvalidated at current sample sizes. Cocoa's +266% medium-horizon figure is still the board's most eye-catching number and its weakest, resting on essentially one documented event. Soybeans SHORT remains the only validated thesis (five of five leave-one-out cases). Paper 26's null result on the broad market is still enforced by construction — no index, VIX, dollar, or rates exposure exists in this system, and none should. Calibration must still be earned, event by event; the three new rows simply give the engine the signals the research had already identified as its best.

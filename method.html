Thesis

A research instrument that grades its own homework

The system converts a physical anomaly into ranked, probabilistic, fully-attributed market theses — and then keeps score against reality, in public, forever. Data refreshes once a day (NOAA ONI and futures closes); each run re-derives the entire board from scratch and grades every sealed forecast whose horizon has closed. Ten commitments carry the design:

  1. 01 · The evidence base

    Twenty-nine papers, twenty-three events, a hundred years. The corpus yields a reliability gradient: a signal is only as good as supply concentration × a narrow weather-critical growth window × inelastic demand.

    Fishmeal responded in 5 of 5 events (+60–250%): Peruvian upwelling collapses, quota cuts follow, with 4–6 months of lead. Palm oil — 85% of supply in Indonesia and Malaysia — rose +75% in 1997-98 on a physiological 6–12 month lag. Coffee moves on Brazil and Vietnam flowering droughts, with prices leading the ONI peak by 2–6 months. Soybeans drop, counterintuitively: El Niño wets Argentina and southern Brazil (~65% of world exports) into glut. Natural gas is the most robust energy signal — milder North American winters, −17 to −23%.

  2. 02 · The nulls are load-bearing

    The S&P 500 showed no El Niño fingerprint in any of five events — each was owned by the Fed, by crises, or by tech. So this system holds no index, VIX, dollar or rates exposure, ever.

    The completeness ledger rules assets OUT with reasons — lumber is housing, gold is macro, aluminium is China demand — and every asset gets a verdict.

  3. 03 · Timing is structural

    ENSO is forecastable 6–18 months out (skill 0.65–0.76 at six months, with a Feb–May “spring barrier”). Historically ~80% of the commodity move was available at the April–June watch window; waiting for official confirmation left 20–30%. Grains respond late and inventory-buffered.

  4. 04 · Analogs, phase-aligned

    Each event is a state vector — intensity, trajectory, duration, onset seasonality — matched by weighted Mahalanobis distance and multiplied by macro-regime agreement (rates, dollar, inflation, demand). Month two of the present is compared to month two of the past, never to another event's peak. The ensemble is weighted, never a single chosen precedent — and the active event never matches itself.

  5. 05 · Distributions, not calls

    Forecasts are analog-weighted empirical distributions of historical outcomes: percentiles, not points. Target probabilities shrink toward 50% as effective sample size falls. Certainty is unrepresentable in this arithmetic — a system that can always say 100% eventually will.

  6. 06 · Disagreement is data

    When historical response and live signals point in opposite directions, the conflict is adjudicated by evidence weight and displayed — never averaged into false agreement.

    Corn is the live example: its history says down while its live signals lean up, and paper 08 shows why both are real — the US growing season misses the El Niño peak, and announcement spikes fade. The badge shows the fight.

  7. 07 · Lineage everywhere

    Every displayed number resolves to its evidence: a signal, a sourced historical response, an analog contribution, a paper, a timestamp. A missing value renders as missing. Nothing is interpolated into existence.

  8. 08 · Forecasts are contracts

    Each prediction is frozen into an append-only ledger with a direction, magnitude, probability, benchmark, invalidation condition, and end date. When the horizon closes, it is graded against realized prices. Rewriting a sealed contract is a fatal error by construction.

    Envelopes are keyed per engine version — a new engine seals its own view of history; prior versions' envelopes remain forever.

  9. 09 · How the backtest works

    1. a) Leave-one-event-out. For every commodity × event, the engine is rebuilt as if that event never happened — catalog, standardization and covariance all excluded — then made to predict it cold.
    2. b) Grading. Direction right? A HIT means the realized move reached at least half the predicted magnitude. The Brier score penalizes confidence itself — predicting 80% and missing costs more than predicting 55% and missing; a coin scores 0.25.
    3. c) Calibration slope asks whether stated probabilities track observed frequencies: when it says 70%, does reality behave like 70%?
    4. d) The gate. Validated = Brier beats the coin by ≥1 point AND slope ≥ 0.8 AND at least 5 graded cases. A spectacular Brier on 3 samples stays unvalidated, because sample size is part of the gate.
    5. e) The time machine re-runs history with leakage impossible by construction, against four naive baselines — always-long-in-El-Niño, strong-events-only, seasonality, coin — plus a kill test that deletes the analog information to measure what similarity actually buys.

  10. 10 · The limits, stated plainly

    Three instrumental super-events exist in the entire record. Cocoa's celebrated +266% rests on one documented event that was half disease and market structure. Pre-1950 reconstructions carry roughly 2× uncertainty — 1925-26 was a coastal event, not ONI-comparable. 2023-24 hinted that a warming climate can decouple ocean forcing from market response. Price snapshots begin in 2000.

    The roadmap follows the evidence: the corpus's most reliable signals — fishmeal, palm oil, rice — are not yet on the board. Extending the catalog to them is worth more than any new mathematics.

In one breath: the ocean runs a fever → find which past fever today most resembles → look up what prices did those times → state a careful, never-certain expectation → seal it → grade it when reality arrives.
